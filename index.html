SHANK3: Treatment Options and Research

Evidence-informed overview for families and clinicians

TL;DR

There is no approved disease-modifying therapy for SHANK3-related conditions (often associated with Phelan-McDermid syndrome). Care is currently supportive and symptom-directed, while multiple clinical trials are ongoing.

This page is for educational purposes only and does not replace medical advice. Please consult a clinical geneticist and your care team for personalized guidance.

Recommended Care Path (Now)

  • Clinical genetics/neurology evaluation to confirm diagnosis, counsel, and coordinate care.
  • Multidisciplinary supports: speech/language therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, behavioral supports (ABA or alternatives as appropriate).
  • Education planning: individualized education program (IEP) or 504 plan where applicable.
  • Symptom monitoring: sleep, feeding, gastrointestinal issues, seizures, behavior/irritability, anxiety, ADHD features.

Symptom-Directed Treatments (Standard Practice)

  • Behavior/irritability: in ASD, risperidone or aripiprazole can help irritability; dosing and monitoring by a physician are essential.
  • Anxiety/mood: SSRIs/SNRIs may be considered under clinician supervision.
  • ADHD symptoms: stimulants or non-stimulants per standard guidelines.
  • Sleep: sleep hygiene; melatonin is commonly used, clinician-guided dosing.
  • Seizures: treat per epilepsy standards with anti-seizure medications when indicated.
  • GI/feeding: reflux/constipation management; feeding therapy as needed.

Management should be individualized. See references and professional guidelines for details.

Emerging and Investigational Approaches

  • IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor-1): early small trials in Phelan-McDermid/SHANK3 suggest potential social/behavioral benefits; still investigational.
  • Oxytocin, intranasal insulin, bumetanide: mixed/limited evidence; research ongoing.
  • Gene-targeted strategies (e.g., gene replacement, upregulation): preclinical/early-stage; not yet standard clinical care.

These options are generally available only via clinical trials. Discuss eligibility and risks with your care team.
